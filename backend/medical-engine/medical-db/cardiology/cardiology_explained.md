## Chapter 1: Cardiac Arrest & Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation

### 1.1 Introduction & The Chain of Survival
Cardiac arrest represents the abrupt cessation of mechanical cardiac pump activity, resulting in global cerebral and tissue hypoperfusion. Survival depends on the rapid execution of the **Chain of Survival**:
1. Early recognition and immediate call for emergency medical services
2. Early high-quality Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)
3. Rapid defibrillation for shockable rhythms
4. Advanced resuscitation and integrated post-cardiac arrest care

### 1.2 Basic Life Support (BLS) Standards
| Parameter | Standard Adult Specification | Pediatric Specification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Compression Rate** | $100–120\text{ compressions/min}$ | $100–120\text{ compressions/min}$ |
| **Compression Depth** | $5–6\text{ cm}$ ($2–2.4\text{ inches}$) with full recoil | At least $1/3$ AP chest diameter ($\sim 4–5\text{ cm}$) |
| **Ratio (No Advanced Airway)**| $30\text{ compressions} : 2\text{ breaths}$ | $30:2$ (single rescuer) or $15:2$ (two rescuers) |
| **Ventilation (Advanced Airway)**| $1\text{ breath every } 6\text{ seconds}$ ($10\text{ breaths/min}$) | $1\text{ breath every } 2–3\text{ seconds}$ ($20–30\text{ breaths/min}$) |
| **Pulse Check Duration** | $\le 10\text{ seconds}$ (Carotid artery) | $\le 10\text{ seconds}$ (Brachial in infant, Carotid/Femoral in child) |

### 1.3 Advanced Life Support (ALS) Algorithm

```
                          [ CARDIAC ARREST ]
                                  │
                          [ Attach Monitor ]
                                  │
             ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
             ▼                                         ▼
    [ Shockable Rhythm ]                     [ Non-Shockable Rhythm ]
  (VF / Pulseless VT)                            (Asystole / PEA)
             │                                         │
    • 1 Shock (150-200J Biphasic)              • Resume CPR immediately (2 min)
    • Resume CPR immediately (2 min)           • IV/IO Epinephrine 1 mg ASAP,
    • IV/IO Epinephrine 1 mg every 3-5 min       then every 3-5 min
    • Amiodarone 300 mg bolus after 3rd shock  • Identify and treat 4 Hs & 4 Ts
      (repeat 150 mg after 5th shock)
```

### 1.4 Table 1.1 — Reversible Causes of Cardiac Arrest (The 4 Hs and 4 Ts)

| Etiology | Pathophysiological Mechanism | Diagnostic Clues | Emergency Targeted Intervention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypoxia** | Asphyxiation, severe airway obstruction, ARDS | Cyanosis, low $\text{SpO}_2$, loss of ETCO2 | Endotracheal intubation, $100\%\text{ FiO}_2$, bag-valve mask ventilation |
| **Hypovolemia** | Massive hemorrhage, severe dehydration, third-spacing | Flat neck veins, pale skin, trauma history | Rapid crystalloid infusion ($1–2\text{ L}$), uncrossmatched O-negative blood |
| **Hyperkalemia / Hypokalemia** | Potassium disturbance altering membrane potential | Peaked T waves, wide QRS / Flattened T, U waves | 10% Calcium Gluconate $10\text{ mL}$, Insulin + Dextrose, $\text{KCl}$ infusion |
| **Hypothermia** | Core body temp $< 30^\circ\text{C}$ causing enzyme failure | Cold exposure, J (Osborn) waves on ECG | Active internal rewarming (warmed IV fluids, peritoneal lavage, ECMO) |
| **Thrombosis (Coronary)** | Acute coronary occlusion causing VF/VT | Preceding angina, ST-elevation on pre-arrest ECG | Emergent primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) |
| **Thrombosis (Pulmonary)** | Saddle pulmonary embolism obstructing RV outflow | History of DVT, unexplained PEA, distended JVP | Systemic thrombolysis (Alteplase $50\text{ mg}$ or Tenecteplase) |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | Pleural air accumulation compressing great veins | Tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds, hyperresonance | Immediate needle thoracostomy followed by intercostal chest drain |
| **Tamponade (Cardiac)** | Pericardial fluid obstructing diastolic filling | Distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds, trauma | Emergent ultrasound-guided subxiphoid pericardiocentesis |
| **Toxins / Overdose** | Drug toxicity (opioids, beta-blockers, CCBs, TCAs) | Pinpoint pupils, prolonged QTc, wide QRS | Naloxone, Flumazenil, Calcium, Glucagon, High-dose Insulin, Sodium Bicarbonate |

### 2.1 The Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP) Waveform

```
Pressure
  ▲
  │        a wave
  │        ┌──┐             v wave
  │       ╱    ╲           ┌──┐
  │      ╱      ╲    c    ╱    ╲
  │     ╱        ╲  ┌─┐  ╱      ╲
  │    ╱          ╲╱   ╲╱        ╲
  │   ╱           x descent       ╲
  │  ╱                             ╲
  └──┴──────────────────────────────┴────────► Time
                                   y descent
```

### Table 2.1 — JVP Waveform Components, Mechanics & Abnormalities

| Waveform Component | Cardiac Cycle Timing | Underlying Mechanical Event | Pathological Abnormalities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **a wave** | End-diastole (Pre-S1) | Active right atrial contraction into RV | • **Absent**: Atrial Fibrillation<br>• **Giant a wave**: Pulmonary HTN, Tricuspid Stenosis<br>• **Cannon a wave**: Complete Heart Block, VT |
| **c wave** | Early systole (Isovolumetric) | Bulging of closed tricuspid valve into RA | Normal physiological transient |
| **x descent** | Mid-systole | Downward displacement of tricuspid valve + RA relaxation | • **Exaggerated**: Cardiac Tamponade, Constrictive Pericarditis<br>• **Reversed / Blunted**: Tricuspid Regurgitation |
| **v wave** | Late systole | Passive venous filling of RA against closed tricuspid valve | • **Giant Systolic v wave**: Severe Tricuspid Regurgitation (Lancisi's sign) |
| **y descent** | Early diastole (Post-S2) | Rapid passive emptying of RA blood into RV after tricuspid opens | • **Steep / Deep y descent**: Constrictive Pericarditis (Friedreich's sign)<br>• **Slow / Blunted y descent**: Tricuspid Stenosis, Cardiac Tamponade |

### Table 2.2 — Heart Sounds & Auscultatory Characteristics

| Heart Sound | Timing in Cycle | Mechanism of Production | Clinical Significance & Associations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Sound (S1)** | Onset of systole | Mitral ($M_1$) & Tricuspid ($T_1$) closure | • **Loud S1**: Mitral Stenosis with mobile leaflets, tachycardia, short PR<br>• **Soft S1**: Mitral Regurgitation, calcified valve, long PR, severe LV failure |
| **Second Sound (S2)**| Onset of diastole | Aortic ($A_2$) & Pulmonary ($P_2$) closure | • **Physiological Split**: Widens during inspiration, closes in expiration<br>• **Fixed Split**: Ostium Secundum Atrial Septal Defect (ASD)<br>• **Reversed Split**: Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB), Severe Aortic Stenosis |
| **Third Sound (S3)** | Early diastole ($0.14\text{ s}$ post-S2) | Rapid passive blood filling decelerating in compliant ventricle | • Physiological in children and pregnancy<br>• **Pathological Gallop ($> 40\text{y}$)**: Left Ventricular Systolic Heart Failure, MR, VSD |
| **Fourth Sound (S4)**| Late diastole (Pre-S1) | Atrial kick forced into a stiff, non-compliant ventricle | • **Always Pathological**: Hypertensive Heart Disease, Aortic Stenosis, HOCM, Ischemia |

## Chapter 3: Conquering the 12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG)

### 3.1 Systematic 7-Step ECG Analysis
1. **Heart Rate**: $300 / (\text{number of large boxes between R-R})$
2. **Rhythm**: Sinus rhythm (upright P waves in I, II, aVF; consistent PR interval) vs Arrhythmia
3. **Cardiac Electrical Axis**: Evaluated via Leads I and aVF
4. **P Wave Morphology**: Width $< 120\text{ ms}$, amplitude $< 2.5\text{ mm}$
5. **PR Interval**: Normal $120–200\text{ ms}$ ($3–5\text{ small squares}$)
6. **QRS Complex**: Normal width $< 120\text{ ms}$ ($< 3\text{ small squares}$)
7. **ST-Segment & T Wave**: Elevation, depression, inversion, and QTc calculation

### Table 3.1 — 12-Lead ECG Coronary Territory & STEMI Localization

| Anatomical Territory | Contiguous ECG Leads | Culprit Coronary Artery | Reciprocal ST Depression |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Septal** | $V_1, V_2$ | Left Anterior Descending (LAD - Proximal Septal branches) | None |
| **Anterior** | $V_3, V_4$ | Left Anterior Descending (LAD - Diagonal branches) | Inferior leads (II, III, aVF) |
| **Anteroseptal** | $V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4$ | Left Anterior Descending (LAD) | Inferior leads (II, III, aVF) |
| **Extensive Anterior** | $V_1–V_6, \text{Lead I}, \text{aVL}$ | Left Main Coronary Artery (LMCA) or Proximal LAD | Inferior leads (II, III, aVF) |
| **High Lateral** | $\text{Lead I}, \text{aVL}$ | Left Circumflex (LCx) or First Diagonal ($D_1$) LAD | Inferior leads (II, III, aVF) |
| **Inferior** | $\text{Lead II}, \text{Lead III}, \text{aVF}$ | Right Coronary Artery (RCA - 85%) or LCx (15%) | High Lateral leads (I, aVL) |
| **Posterior** | $V_7, V_8, V_9$ (Confirmed) | Distal LCx or Posterior Descending Artery (PDA) | Tall R waves and ST depression in $V_1–V_3$ |
| **Right Ventricular** | $V_3R, V_4R$ | Proximal Right Coronary Artery (RCA) | Coexists with Inferior STEMI |
