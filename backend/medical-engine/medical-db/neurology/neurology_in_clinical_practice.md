## Section I: Principles of Clinical Neurodiagnosis & Localization

### 1.1 The Systematic Neurological Examination
1. **Mental Status & Higher Cortical Functions**: Level of consciousness (Glasgow Coma Scale), orientation, attention, memory, language (Broca vs Wernicke aphasia), praxis, and executive function.
2. **Cranial Nerves (CN I to XII)**: Visual acuity and visual fields (CN II), pupillary light reflexes (CN II, III), extraocular movements (CN III, IV, VI), facial sensation & corneal reflex (CN V), facial symmetry & expression (CN VII), hearing & vestibular balance (CN VIII), palate elevation & gag reflex (CN IX, X), trapezius/sternocleidomastoid strength (CN XI), tongue protrusion & fasciculations (CN XII).
3. **Motor System**: Muscle bulk (atrophy/hypertrophy), tone (spasticity, rigidity, hypotonia), power grading ($0–5$ Medical Research Council scale), pronator drift.
4. **Reflexes**: Deep tendon reflexes ($0$ absent, $1+$ hypoactive, $2+$ normal, $3+$ hyperactive, $4+$ clonus), pathological reflexes (Babinski sign, Hoffmann sign).
5. **Sensory System**: Pinprick (spinothalamic), temperature (spinothalamic), vibration ($128\text{ Hz}$ tuning fork - dorsal column), light touch, proprioception/joint position sense (dorsal column), cortical sensations (stereognosis, graphesthesia, two-point discrimination).
6. **Coordination & Cerebellum**: Finger-to-nose, heel-to-shin, rapid alternating movements (dysdiadochokinesia), rebound phenomenon, intention tremor.
7. **Stance & Gait**: Normal gait, tandem gait, Romberg test, heel-and-toe walking.

### Table 1.1 — Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) vs Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) Lesions

| Clinical Feature | Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) Lesion | Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) Lesion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site of Lesion** | Cerebral cortex, internal capsule, brainstem, corticospinal tract | Anterior horn cells, motor nerve root, peripheral nerve, plexus |
| **Muscle Bulk / Trophism** | Disuse atrophy only (late and mild) | Severe, early, marked neurogenic muscle wasting/atrophy |
| **Muscle Tone** | **Spasticity** (Velocity-dependent "clasp-knife" hypertonia) | **Hypotonia / Flaccidity** (Decreased or absent resistance) |
| **Deep Tendon Reflexes** | **Hyperreflexia** ($3+$ or $4+$) with sustained clonus | **Hyporeflexia or Areflexia** ($0$ or $1+$) |
| **Plantar Reflex** | **Extensor (Positive Babinski sign)**: Dorsiflexion of great toe | **Flexor (Normal)**: Plantarflexion of toes, or mute |
| **Fasciculations** | **Absent** | **Frequently Present** (Visible spontaneous motor unit twitches) |
| **Pattern of Weakness** | Pyramidal distribution (Upper limbs: extensors $<$ flexors; Lower limbs: flexors $<$ extensors) | Segmental, radicular, or peripheral nerve distribution |

## Section II: Cerebrovascular Diseases & Stroke

### 2.1 Acute Ischemic Stroke & Emergency Protocols
- **Definition**: Sudden focal neurological deficit resulting from inadequate cerebral arterial blood supply causing ischemic infarction.
- **National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS)**: $0–42$ scale measuring stroke severity:
  - $0$: No stroke symptoms
  - $1–4$: Minor stroke
  - $5–15$: Moderate stroke
  - $16–20$: Moderate-to-severe stroke
  - $21–42$: Severe stroke

### Table 2.1 — Acute Ischemic Stroke Revascularization Guidelines

| Revascularization Modality | Eligibility Time Window | Mandatory Inclusion Criteria | Key Exclusion Criteria | Dosing / Technique |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intravenous Thrombolysis (IV rtPA / Alteplase)**| **$0–4.5\text{ hours}$** from symptom onset / last known normal | • Clinical diagnosis of ischemic stroke with measurable deficit<br>• Age $\ge 18\text{ years}$<br>• NCCT brain excludes hemorrhage | • Intracranial hemorrhage on CT<br>• Active internal bleeding<br>• Ischemic stroke/severe head trauma in past 3 months<br>• Platelets $< 100,000/\mu\text{L}$<br>• INR $> 1.7$ or on DOAC $< 48\text{h}$<br>• BP $> 185/110\text{ mmHg}$ refractory to IV labetalol | **$0.9\text{ mg/kg}$** (Max $90\text{ mg}$):<br>• $10\%$ given as IV bolus over $1\text{ min}$<br>• Remaining $90\%$ infused over $60\text{ min}$ |
| **Endovascular Thrombectomy (EVT)** | **$0–6\text{ hours}$** (Up to $24\text{ hours}$ with DAWN / DEFUSE-3 mismatch criteria) | • Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO) in anterior circulation (ICA or M1 segment MCA)<br>• Baseline $\text{mRS} \le 1$<br>• $\text{ASPECTS} \ge 6$ on non-contrast CT | • Extensive core infarct ($> 1/3$ MCA territory or ASPECTS $< 6$)<br>• Severe baseline pre-stroke disability ($\text{mRS} \ge 3$) | Direct mechanical clot retrieval using Stent Retriever and/or Aspiration Catheter |

## Section III: Epilepsy & Paroxysmal Disorders

### 3.1 International League Against Epilepsy (ILAE 2017) Classification
- **Focal Seizures**: Originating within neuronal networks limited to one cerebral hemisphere:
  - *Focal Aware*: Consciousness intact.
  - *Focal Impaired Awareness*: Consciousness compromised (automatisms, confusion).
  - *Focal to Bilateral Tonic-Clonic*: Secondary generalization.
- **Generalized Seizures**: Originating at some point within and rapidly engaging bilaterally distributed cerebral networks:
  - *Absence*: Sudden brief lapse of awareness ($3\text{ Hz}$ spike-and-wave on EEG).
  - *Generalized Tonic-Clonic (GTC)*: Tonic extension followed by rhythmic clonic jerking.
  - *Myoclonic, Atonic (Drop attacks), Tonic*.

### Table 3.1 — Status Epilepticus Emergency Treatment Protocol

| Stage / Phase | Time Elapsed | First-Line Emergency Antiseizure Regimen | Alternative / Second-Line Options |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Early Status** (Emergent Initial Therapy) | **$0–5\text{ minutes}$** | **IV Lorazepam $4\text{ mg}$** (at $2\text{ mg/min}$, repeat once at $5\text{ min}$) OR **IV Diazepam $10\text{ mg}$** | **IM Midazolam $10\text{ mg}$** (if no IV access) or Rectal Diazepam $10–20\text{ mg}$ |
| **Phase 2: Established Status** (Urgent Control Therapy) | **$5–20\text{ minutes}$** | **IV Levetiracetam $60\text{ mg/kg}$** (Max $4500\text{ mg}$) OR **IV Fosphenytoin $20\text{ mg PE/kg}$** (Max $1500\text{ mg PE}$) | **IV Sodium Valproate $40\text{ mg/kg}$** (Max $3000\text{ mg}$) or IV Lacosamide $400\text{ mg}$ |
| **Phase 3: Refractory Status** (General Anesthesia & ICU) | **$> 20–40\text{ minutes}$** | **Continuous IV Midazolam** (Load $0.2\text{ mg/kg}$, infusion $0.05–2\text{ mg/kg/h}$) OR **Continuous IV Propofol** (Load $1–2\text{ mg/kg}$, infusion $1–5\text{ mg/kg/h}$) | **IV Ketamine infusion** or IV Thiopental / Pentobarbital (Titrated to burst suppression on continuous EEG) |

## Section IV: Dementias & Neurodegenerative Disorders

### Table 4.1 — Differential Diagnosis of Major Dementia Syndromes

| Dementia Subtype | Primary Neuropathology | Cognitive & Behavioral Profile | Motor & Neurological Features | Characteristic Biomarkers & Imaging |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alzheimer's Disease (AD)** | Extracellular $\beta$-amyloid ($A\beta_{42}$) plaques & intracellular hyperphosphorylated Tau neurofibrillary tangles | Early short-term episodic memory deficit $\rightarrow$ visuospatial disorientation, anomia | Late-stage motor slowing, myoclonus | MRI: Medial temporal lobe & Hippocampal atrophy; CSF: Low $A\beta_{42}$, High Total & Phospho-Tau |

## Section V: Neuro-Immunological & Demyelinating Diseases

### 5.1 Multiple Sclerosis (McDonald Criteria 2017 Revision)
- **Dissemination in Space (DIS)**: Demonstrated by $\ge 1$ T2-hyperintense lesion in $\ge 2$ of the 4 typical CNS areas:
  1. Periventricular
  2. Cortical or Juxtacortical
  3. Infratentorial (Brainstem or Cerebellum)
  4. Spinal Cord
- **Dissemination in Time (DIT)**: Demonstrated by:
  - Simultaneous presence of asymptomatic gadolinium-enhancing and non-enhancing lesions on any single MRI, OR
  - A new T2 or gadolinium-enhancing lesion on follow-up MRI, OR
  - Presence of **$\ge 2$ CSF-specific Oligoclonal Bands (OCBs)** matching clinical DIS.

### Table 5.1 — Multiple Sclerosis (MS) vs Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD)

| Diagnostic Parameter | Multiple Sclerosis (MS) | Neuromyelitis Optica (NMOSD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathophysiology** | Autoimmune T-cell mediated demyelination of CNS myelin | Autoimmune astrocytopathy with secondary demyelination |
| **Primary Autoantibody** | None (Oligoclonal bands present in $> 90\%$ of CSF) | **Aquaporin-4 IgG (AQP4-IgG)** in serum ($> 75\%$) |
| **Optic Neuritis** | Typically unilateral, partial visual loss, retrobulbar | Bilateral, severe, altitudinal visual loss, chiasmal involvement |
| **Spinal Cord Lesion (Myelitis)**| Short-segment T2 lesions ($< 2$ vertebral segments), peripheral/dorsal | **Longitudinally Extensive Transverse Myelitis (LETM)** ($\ge 3$ vertebral segments, central cord) |
| **Brain MRI** | Dawson's fingers perpendicular to ventricles, juxtacortical lesions | Area postrema lesions (intractable nausea/vomiting/hiccups) |
| **Acute Relapse Treatment** | High-dose IV Methylprednisolone ($1\text{ g/day}$ for $3–5\text{ days}$) | High-dose IV Methylprednisolone $\rightarrow$ Early Plasma Exchange (PLEX) |

## Section VI: Neuromuscular Junction & Peripheral Nerve Disorders

### Table 6.1 — Myasthenia Gravis (MG) vs Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS)

| Feature | Myasthenia Gravis (MG) | Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Target Autoantigen** | **Post-synaptic Acetylcholine Receptor (AChR)** (85%) or Muscle-Specific Kinase (MuSK) | **Pre-synaptic Voltage-Gated Calcium Channels (P/Q-type VGCC)** ($> 90\%$) |
| **Malignancy Association** | **Thymoma** (15%) or Thymic Hyperplasia (65%) | **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)** ($> 60\%$, Paraneoplastic) |
| **Weakness Pattern** | Ocular (ptosis, diplopia) in $> 85\%$, bulbar, proximal limbs; **Worsens with exertion/end of day** | Proximal lower limbs (difficulty rising from chair); **Improves temporarily with exercise (Post-tetanic potentiation)** |
| **Deep Tendon Reflexes** | **Normal** (Preserved) | **Hypoactive or Absent** (May return after brief maximal contraction) |
| **Autonomic Symptoms** | **Absent** | **Present** (Dry mouth, erectile dysfunction, constipation) |
| **Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS)**| **Decremental response** ($> 10\%$ drop at low-frequency $3\text{ Hz}$) | **Incremental response** ($> 100–200\%$ increase at high-frequency $20–50\text{ Hz}$) |
| **First-Line Pharmacotherapy**| Pyridostigmine (AChE inhibitor) + Corticosteroids / Thymectomy | Amifampridine (3,4-DAP) + Treat underlying SCLC |
